About

How a 12-year search and period of chronic pain helped me develop a tool for internal transformation.

01

The Accidental Extrovert

You know that weird kid in the back of the class whose only friends were obscure video games and TV shows? That was me.

But almost overnight, something clicked, and without a single useful self-help book, I became a completely different person.

Suddenly, I was doing standup comedy, hosting networking events, and fast-tracking my way up at Morgan Stanley.

Within 18 months, I was pulled into a veteran SME group, eventually running corporate training and serving as my office's Toastmasters President. I had transformed into a highly capable professional, but it bothered me deeply that I had absolutely no idea how I did it.

02

The $100,000 Problem

Determined to reverse-engineer my own spontaneous change, I spent the next 12 years and $100,000 traveling the world to learn from the best coaches, philosophers, and leadership experts I could find.

At the same time, I was testing these concepts live, logging over 5,000 hours of coaching, 1:1 and in groups, across the corporate world, Toastmasters, peer groups and my private practice.

12years of research

Between the high-end global workshops and my own training rooms, I noticed a bleak common thread: only about 1 in 20 were still working on something that mattered to them 6 months later.

Passionate people who were in an environment that allowed them to be fully honest with themselves and what matters most, but were forced to watch it fade once they were home.

The secret to lasting change completely eluded me, until a physical crisis forced my hand.

03

The Painful Paradigm Shift

Shortly after returning from a workshop in Europe, a childhood condition flared up, triggering an eight-year cycle of debilitating chronic pain.

Suddenly, working and training from home meant managing intense physical stress, and I quickly learned that what I wanted and cared about no longer mattered. I just wanted the pain to stop.

But during the flare-ups, I noticed something strange: some days, I could host a massive corporate training through the pain with a genuine smile.

On other days, with the exact same pain, sleep, and food, the smallest inconvenience derailed me.

I began to notice when my internal compass shifted: the signs in my nervous system and belief system that led to a compromised state and disrupted my ability to choose what came next.

04

Finding True North

I started simply tracking the thoughts, feelings and assumptions that would trigger me into a compromised state. I discovered it was almost never the pain itself.

When doctors finally helped me resolve the pain, I continued to watch my internal compass.

I got the first mid-year raise on my team in over 15 years, simply by continually adjusting my internal compass.

I began coaching others to recognize these aspects of themselves. One after another, people who had spent a decade and $50k+ on personal development were overcoming stubborn patterns that had survived every past attempt.

The results were undeniable. Not only did I find a way to keep myself pointed at what matters most to me, but I successfully helped friends, colleagues, and strangers do so as well.

And that is exactly why you're here today.
